About The Independent Explorer

Some people plan their holidays six months in advance. I respect those people enormously and have never once been one of them. I'm The Independent Explorer — a Londoner with a well-worn passport, an unreasonable number of frequent flyer miles, and a genuine belief that the best travel writing is honest about the parts that don't make the highlight reel. I've been travelling seriously for the better part of fifteen years, across six continents and more countries than I can list without sounding like I'm showing off. I've trekked at altitude and eaten at Michelin-starred restaurants and slept in dormitory bunks and glass-roofed cabins above the Arctic Circle. I've taken the slow boat when I should have flown and flown when I should have taken the slow boat. I've made most of the mistakes worth making, and a few that weren't. What I've learned from all of it is that there is no single right way to travel. Budget backpacking and boutique hotels, solo adventures and trips built around food or history or wellness — every style has its place, depending on who you are and what you need from the road at any given moment. The Independent Explorer exists because I wanted a space that reflects that breadth honestly, without the rose-tinted filter that so much travel content defaults to. You won't find perfect itineraries here. You'll find real accounts — the altitude headache at 5,100 metres, the two nights of cloud cover before the northern lights finally appeared, the retreat diet that tested my resolve somewhere around day three of kitchari. The good stuff too, of course — always the good stuff. But the full picture, as faithfully as I can render it. I'm based in London, which means I have easy access to Europe and a very good reason to spend as much time as possible somewhere else. I write every post on this site myself, from notes taken in the moment and memories I'm not ready to let go of yet. If something I've written helps you plan a trip, or convinces you to book one you'd been putting off, then this site is doing exactly what it's supposed to. The world is large and mostly wonderful. Go and see it. — The Independent Explorer
